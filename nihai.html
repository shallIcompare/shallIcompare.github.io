NIHAI

Norms of Assertion in Linguistic Human–AI Interaction. A four-country research consortium funded by HERA/CHANSE (2025–2028).

The problem

Misinformation, fake news and conspiracy theories spread widely, and trust in the media, science and public institutions has eroded as a result. Digital communication has made this worse. We expect it to get worse still, because many of our conversations will soon be with AI systems rather than with people. NIHAI asks what principles such systems should follow, and tests the answers across cultures.

The research

The project combines ethics, linguistics, media studies and social computing. It studies three things across several European and non-European languages: what people expect from an AI system in conversation, how they judge a system that breaks those expectations, and what follows for their willingness to rely on it. We examine how context, role, stakes and the perceived agency of the system shape these judgements. The results feed into design principles for conversational agents, which we test on Reporter, the AI-driven journalism application of our partner Polaris News.

Partners and funding

Markus Kneer (project leader), University of Graz · Markus Christen (PI), University of Zurich · Michaela Constantinescu (PI), University of Bucharest · Izabela Skoczeń (PI), Jagiellonian University, Kraków. Polaris News, led by the journalist Hannes Grassegger, is a key collaborator.

The project is funded with over one million euros by the HERA/CHANSE programme, with national contributions from the participating countries’ research councils.
