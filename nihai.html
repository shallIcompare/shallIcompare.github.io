NIHAI

Norms of Assertion in Linguistic Human–AI Interaction. A four-country research consortium funded by HERA/CHANSE (2025–2028).

The problem

The widespread circulation of misinformation, disinformation, fake news and conspiracy theories is eroding trust in the media, science and government institutions. We regard this communication crisis as one of the central crises of our time. Digital communication has made it worse, and we predict that it will intensify further because many of our conversations in the near future will be with AI systems.

NIHAI studies the principles that should govern how conversational AI systems communicate. Through detailed research across cultures, we aim to help prevent or limit a further communication crisis fuelled by AI.

The research

The project draws on ethics, linguistics, media and communication studies, and social computer science. We study conversations between people and AI systems across several European and non-European languages and cultures.

  1. We examine what people think AI systems should and should not say.
  2. We study how people judge systems that violate these expectations, and how those judgements affect subsequent conversations.
  3. We examine factors that may change these relationships, such as the context, the roles of those taking part, the complexity of the AI model and the stakes. We also study processes that may help explain the relationships, such as whether people perceive the system as an agent or attribute a mind to it.
  4. We investigate how these factors affect people’s evaluations of conversational AI systems and their willingness to rely on them, and how this varies across cultures.
  5. We use the findings to propose principles for the responsible design and use of conversational AI systems. We test these principles directly in Reporter, the AI-driven lay-journalism application from our associated partner Polaris News.

Partners and funding

Markus Kneer at the University of Graz leads the project. The other principal investigators are Markus Christen at the University of Zurich, Mihaela Constantinescu at the University of Bucharest and Izabela Skoczeń at Jagiellonian University in Kraków. Polaris News, led by the award-winning journalist Hannes Grassegger, is a key collaborator.

The HERA/CHANSE programme provides more than one million euros in funding for the project.
